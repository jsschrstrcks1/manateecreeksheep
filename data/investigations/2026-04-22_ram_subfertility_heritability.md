# Ram Subfertility — Heritability, Inheritance Mode, and Daughter Risk

**Date:** 2026-04-22
**Triggered by:** Owner decision to retain largest Elsie triplet (sired by
Eclipse, a ram who bred only 1 of 6 ewes in a full year) conditional on her
first-lambing performance.
**Sources:** Perplexity (research-oriented), You.com Research API. Costs: $0.013.

## TL;DR

- **Mode of inheritance: polygenic** — not a simple dominant or recessive
  gene. Subfertility is the lower tail of a multifactorial quantitative
  trait distribution.
- **It IS heritable.** Ram mating success h² ≈ 0.30; scrotal circumference
  (SC) h² ≈ 0.25–0.33; libido h² ≈ 0.22; semen quality h² ≈ 0.08–0.17.
- **Daughters of subfertile sires** are, on average, also less fertile.
  Genetic correlation of ram SC with ewe ovulation rate is **+0.20**;
  this is the established basis for using ram SC as indirect selection on
  daughter fertility (Rambouillet data, backed by extension/NSIP).
- **Most likely non-genetic cause** of 1-in-6 breeding success is
  **chronic epididymitis**, usually *Brucella ovis*. Without a breeding
  soundness exam (BSE) + *B. ovis* serology, genetic vs. acquired can't
  be partitioned.
- **The conditional-cull strategy IS defensible** for a small flock but
  is the higher-risk path vs. outright culling the daughter.

## 1. Genetic mechanism — polygenic, not dominant/recessive

Ram fertility is a **classic quantitative (polygenic) trait**, not
controlled by a single dominant or recessive gene. Reviews emphasize that
testis size, libido, semen quality, and mating success are each
partially heritable under multifactorial (polygenic) control, modulated
by environment, management, and disease.

**Practical implication:** you cannot treat Eclipse's subfertility as a
single hidden recessive allele that a daughter either "carries" or
doesn't. She inherits a random half of his polygenic load, on a
continuous distribution. On average she will sit somewhere between him
and the flock mean.

Unlike female prolificacy, which has well-characterized single-gene
effects (FecB / Booroola, BMP15, GDF9), **no equivalent named genes for
ram subfertility** were identified in the literature reviewed.

## 2. Heritability of male fertility traits in sheep

| Trait | h² | Source / breed |
|---|---|---|
| **Ram mating success** (lambs sired in multi-sire) | **0.30 ± 0.12** | Review; moderate, repeatable |
| Scrotal circumference (SC) | 0.25–0.33 avg (0–0.75 range across 40 studies) | Rambouillet, multiple |
| Libido / serving capacity | 0.22 ± 0.04 (repeatability 0.72) | US Sheep Experiment Station |
| Serves per test (Merino) | 0.33 | Merino |
| Ejaculate volume | 0.08–0.17 | Large Australian AI dataset |
| Post-thaw motility | 0.08–0.17 | Australian AI dataset |
| Ewe fertility (lambed vs. not) | 0.07–0.11 | Dorset-Rambouillet-Finn composite |

**Interpretation:** the most economically important trait (ram mating
success) is the *most* heritable at ~0.30. A ram who sits at the low end
of the distribution — which Eclipse does, dramatically — is likely
transmitting below-average fertility genetics, not just having a bad year.

## 3. Risk to daughters — cross-sex genetic correlation

This is the critical piece for the Eclipse-daughter decision.

| Correlation | Value |
|---|---|
| SC × ovulation rate (Rambouillet) | **+0.20** |
| SC × litter size (Rambouillet) | −0.25 |
| Sire SC / mating success → daughter fertility | Favorable; used in NSIP |

**Translation:** daughters of sires with large SC and good mating
success are **more fertile on average**. The inverse applies: daughters
of severely subfertile sires are **expected to be less fertile** than
daughters of high-fertility sires.

The effect is moderate, not absolute. A single daughter could be fine
by chance. But across many daughters the mean shifts downward.

## 4. Sons of subfertile rams

For sons, the heritability is direct (son inherits the same polygenic
fertility architecture). Extension materials explicitly recommend NOT
retaining sons of subfertile rams for breeding, or at minimum BSE-testing
them and withholding heavy use until field fertility is confirmed.

For Eclipse's only son (the black ram triplet, already gifted off-farm),
this is moot — but worth remembering for any future accidental breedings.

## 5. Most likely cause of Eclipse's 1-in-6 year

Extension sources agree: **chronic epididymitis is the single most
common cause** of ram subfertility and sterility. Usually caused by
*Brucella ovis*, sometimes by sperm granuloma, cryptorchidism, or
testicular hypoplasia.

**Diagnostic hierarchy** if you wanted to resolve Eclipse's status before
he leaves:

1. Palpation — enlarged/irregular epididymal tails or soft/small testes
2. *Brucella ovis* serology (blood test)
3. Semen evaluation (motility, concentration, morphology)
4. Scrotal circumference measurement

Since he's going to auction Sunday, this is academic — but if you ever
retain a ram with fertility concerns, BSE before keeping him is the
right move.

## 6. Practical recommendation for the largest Elsie daughter

The reviewed extension literature supports your conditional-cull plan
with specific benchmarks. Copying the defensible version:

**Keep as probationary** — but:

1. **Breed her only to a BSE-proven fertile, unrelated ram.** That means
   Charlie (Pen 3, MC-20), Merrieweather (00016), Buck (TF, MC-2433), or
   the Windlestone Kat/Dorper ram in Pen 2 — all with no fertility red
   flags. Do **not** breed her to any son of Eclipse, or any ram with
   small SC / poor libido history.

2. **Apply strict cull thresholds. Cull if ANY of the following:**
   - Fails to conceive in 1–2 cycles while herdmates conceive
   - Weak/non-vigorous lambs without environmental explanation
   - Repeat singles when flockmates of similar age/genetics are twinning
   - Any perinatal loss without clear cause

3. **Retain only if:**
   - Conceives promptly
   - Lambs unassisted
   - Raises robust lambs to weaning at or above flock average

4. **Never keep her sons as future sires** unless her own performance is
   exceptional AND the sire she's bred to has documented high fertility.
   Even then, BSE any son before using him.

## 7. Net judgment for the flock

- "Keep the largest daughter, cull if she throws bad babies" is
  **defensible and supported by the extension literature**, provided the
  cull triggers are applied without flinching.
- The next-ram decision matters more: since Eclipse sat at the bottom of
  the ram-fertility distribution, the flock benefits most from replacing
  him with a **BSE-tested fertility-proven ram** next time you buy. NSIP
  EBVs for SC or lambs-weaned are the right data to ask for.
- Long-term, this single daughter's line is lower-expected-value than
  lines from your parasite-proven sires (Charlie, Merrieweather, Buck).
  Don't over-invest in breeding her if alternatives exist.
