| Transition | Ewe meets these thresholds to advance |

| **Goose Pen → Pen 3** (purchased ewes only; home-born lambs enter Pen 3 at weaning) | 30d clean: FAMACHA ≤2 every check, no treatment, no B. ovis (if serology available), no footrot |
| **Pen 3 → Pen 5** (Stage 1 → 2) | Conceived in Pen 3, raised lamb to weaning, FAMACHA avg ≤1.50 over the year (at or above flock median), no FAMACHA 4+ events |

| **Pen 1 → Pen 2** (Stage 5 → 6 elite) | FAMACHA avg ≤1.30 (top quartile), consistent twinning, coat_observed=hair or shed-through, lamb weaning weight ≥ flock 75th pct |

Holdback (stay in current pen, no promotion, no cull): met minimum but not advancement threshold. First-parity ewes typically stay one extra year.

Demotion: move to Pen 4 infirmary. Triggers: any FAMACHA 4+ event, one dewormer treatment, lambing problems (dystocia/poor mothering).

Cull (auction or sell): FAMACHA 5 at any stage, open year at stages 3+, 2 consecutive years of demotion, chronic condition unresponsive to treatment.

## Homogenization strategy (debate question d)

**Goal:** a single breed-type — *Manatee Creek Composite* — that is hair-dominant (75%+ hair coat score), parasite-resistant (FAMACHA avg ≤1.40 flock-wide), meaty (60kg+ mature ewe weight), with Awassi milk undertone.

**22 breeds → 1 type** requires multi-generation fixation. The v5 pipeline is the machinery. Here's the strategy:

### 1. Ram side: select the 3-4 genetic profiles you want fixed

The flock's end-state ram profile should be: 50-75% Katahdin (Kelsier line) + 15-25% Dorper (meat frame, culled for parasite susceptibility in subsequent generations) + 10-25% Awassi (milk/hardiness) + trace East Friesian (dairy accent).

**Angus at 50/25/23/2 is almost exactly this profile.** He's the *template*. Use him heavily at Stage 2.
**Charlie's hair composite (Kat/BHD/ABB/WH)** is the other template — slightly more hair-breed heavy, less meat frame. Use him at Stage 1 where volume matters.

**Every other ram is a means to the end** — they exist to produce ewes that then breed back to Angus-type and Charlie-type sires. Over 3-4 generations, the proportion of non-target breed-fractions (Suffolk, Hampshire, Cotswold, Tunis, Jacob, Karakul, GCN, etc.) declines because carriers don't advance.

### 2. Ewe side: fix the Kat-Awassi coefficient

Ewes advancing into Stage 4+ (Tree Fort + Pen 1 + Pen 2) should, by 2030, all carry:
- ≥ 50% Katahdin (from sire or mother)
- ≥ 15% Awassi (milk/hardiness)
- Measurable hair-coat score (observed shedding through summer)

Ewes in earlier stages may carry diverse genetics — they're being tested. Those that pass advance, increasing the breeding population of Kat-Awassi-heavy composites. Those that fail go to Pen 4 or auction.

### 3. Measurement discipline

Homogenization without measurement is wishful thinking. Required:

- **Annual FEC** on all breeding candidates (once a year, ~$5-10/animal at UF vet)
- **FAMACHA** monthly (already happening via the notebook cards)
- **Coat score** annually at peak-shed (July-August Florida) — 1-5 scale for how much hair shedding vs wool retention
- **Lamb weaning weight** per ewe (need scale at weaning, record on the lamb card)
- **Body condition score** twice yearly (breeding + pre-lambing)

Without these, you can't prove homogenization is happening — you're just moving sheep between pens.

### 4. Timeline expectation

- **Year 1 (2026):** pipeline v5 adopted; Angus integrates post-quarantine; first Angus-sired lambs born winter 2026/spring 2027
- **Year 2 (2027):** first Angus daughters wean into Stage 1 Pen 3 under Charlie; Kat content in Pen 1 + 2 stabilizes at ≥50%
- **Year 3 (2028):** first Angus granddaughters (50% of genetic material from Angus line) mature; first true "Manatee Creek Composite" candidates identifiable
- **Year 5 (2030):** ≥80% of Stage 4-6 ewes carry target-profile genetics; pipeline running with minimal outside inputs; homogenization substantially achieved

### 5. What NOT to do

- Don't buy more breeds. You have 22; your job is to consolidate, not diversify further.
- Don't keep lambs from Pen 4 (infirmary) for breeding. The genetics that landed their mother in the infirmary are the ones you're culling against.
- Don't cross-pen breed outside the pipeline unless you have a specific genetic case (e.g., Serendipity White Ram Twin × a test ewe). Every random mating dilutes the fixation progress.
- Don't auction proven Pen 1 or Pen 2 ewes for cash. They're the homogenization engine.
