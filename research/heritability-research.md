# Comprehensive Sheep Heritability (h2) Reference Guide

Compiled from web research across NSIP data, Sheep Genetics Australia parameters, peer-reviewed meta-analyses, extension publications, and individual breed studies. Where ranges exist, a **working estimate** (the central/consensus value most appropriate for breeding prediction formulas) is provided, along with the full range found in the literature.

## WHAT HERITABILITY MEANS FOR PREDICTING OFFSPRING PERFORMANCE

### The Breeder's Equation

**R = h2 x S**

Where:
- **R** = Response to selection (expected genetic change in the next generation)
- **h2** = Heritability (narrow-sense)
- **S** = Selection differential (superiority of selected parents over the population mean)

### Predicting Individual Offspring from a Single Superior Sire

An offspring inherits half its genes from each parent. The expected breeding value of an offspring is:

**Expected offspring BV = (1/2) x Sire BV + (1/2) x Dam BV**

If the dam is average (BV = 0), then:

**Expected offspring BV = (1/2) x Sire BV**

The sire's **Estimated Breeding Value (EBV)** from phenotype:

**Sire EBV = h2 x (Sire phenotype - Population mean)**

### Worked Example: h2 = 0.40, sire is 2 standard deviations above average

Yearling weight, h2 = 0.40. Population mean = 50 kg, SD = 5 kg.

1. Sire is 2 SD above average = 10 kg above the mean (phenotype = 60 kg)
2. Sire EBV = 0.40 x 10 kg = 4.0 kg
3. Offspring gets half: 4.0 / 2 = 2.0 kg
4. **Expected offspring performance = 50 kg + 2.0 kg = 52 kg**

### General Formula

For a sire **d** standard deviations above the mean, mated to an average dam:

**Expected offspring deviation = (h2 x d x SD) / 2**

Or in standard deviation units:

**Expected offspring = (h2 x d) / 2 standard deviations above the mean**

### Practical Impact by Heritability Level

| Sire at +2 SD | h2 = 0.10 (LOW) | h2 = 0.25 (MODERATE) | h2 = 0.40 (HIGH) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offspring expected deviation | +0.10 SD | +0.25 SD | +0.40 SD |

### Important Caveats for Breeding Formulas

1. **EBVs are better than raw phenotypes.** An EBV from NSIP already accounts for heritability, contemporary group effects, known relatives, and marker data. If you have an EBV, use it directly -- do not multiply by h2 again.
2. **Maternal effects matter** for early growth traits. The dam contributes both direct genes and a maternal environment.
3. **Genetic correlations** mean selecting for one trait changes correlated traits.
4. **Generation interval** affects annual genetic progress: Delta-G per year = (h2 x S) / L

**HIGH heritability (h2 > 0.30) -- fast genetic progress possible with phenotypic selection:**
- Fiber diameter (0.60), hair shedding score (0.50), staple length (0.45), fleece weight (0.40), mature body weight (0.40), yearling weight (0.35), scrotal circumference (0.35), ovulation rate (0.35), face cover (0.50), retail cut weight (0.45), gestation length (0.45), breech strike resistance (0.35)

- Birth weight (0.15), weaning weight 60d (0.15), weaning weight 120d (0.25), post-weaning weight (0.30), ADG pre-weaning (0.25), ADG post-weaning (0.25), RFI/feed efficiency (0.27), dressing % (0.30), loin eye area (0.25), fat thickness (0.25), FEC weaning (0.20), FEC post-weaning (0.25), FAMACHA (0.20), foot rot resistance (0.25), age at puberty (0.20), out-of-season breeding (0.15), temperament (0.20), ram libido (0.22), milk production (0.25)

- Litter size (0.10), number weaned (0.05), ewe productivity (0.12), conception rate (0.05), lambing interval (0.06), prolificacy (0.10), lamb survival (0.05), ewe longevity (0.08), mothering ability (0.10), flocking instinct (0.10)
