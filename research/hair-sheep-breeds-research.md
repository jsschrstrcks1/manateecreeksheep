# HAIR SHEEP BREED DATA FOR FLORIDA FLOCKS -- COMPREHENSIVE RESEARCH COMPILATION

Below are structured data tables for each of the nine breeds/types requested, compiled from university extension publications, peer-reviewed research, breed association standards, and USDA data. Where specific quantitative data was unavailable for a trait, that is noted.

---

## 1. KATAHDIN

| Trait | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mature Weight** | Rams: 180--250 lbs; Ewes: 125--180 lbs (Oklahoma State University; KHSI) |
| **Parasite Resistance** | Moderate-high. FEC EBVs range from -100 to +769 within breed (NSIP). Heritability of FEC: h2 = 0.18--0.26 at 60 days, 0.23--0.46 at 120 days. FEC 45% lower than Dorset crosses, 62% lower than Dorper crosses in Virginia Tech trials (Burke et al., 2003). FAMACHA used as indicator trait, correlated with PCV. NSIP recommends culling animals with positive FEC EBVs. |
| **Prolificacy** | Lambing rate ~168%. Mature ewes usually average >2.0 lambs/lambing. Twins common in yearlings; triplets not uncommon. NSIP tracks NLB and NLW EBVs. Annual lambing rate 85% in accelerated programs (VSU study). |
| **Mothering Ability** | Good milkers -- recommended not to wean before 3 months. Ewes lamb easily unassisted. Good maternal behavior; NSIP maternal weaning weight (MWWT) EBV captures milk + mothering. |
| **Temperament** | Docile and calm. Easy to handle for novice shepherds. Occasional flightier individuals due to Wiltshire Horn ancestry in some lines. |
| **Flocking Instinct** | Moderate. Better than some hair breeds but weaker than fine wool breeds. Not suited to open-range production. Small groups manageable. KHSI notes they are not the best "flockers." |
| **Heat Tolerance** | Good. Developed partly from St. Croix (Caribbean origin). Hair coat allows heat dissipation. Perform well in Florida/subtropical climates (UF/IFAS VM264). |
| **Cold Tolerance** | Very good. Developed in Maine. Grow thick winter undercoat (woolly fibers + coarse outer hair) that sheds in spring. Hardy from South America to Canada. Winter foragers -- dig through stockpiled grass. |
| **Coat Type** | Hair coat, variable. Coarse outer hair + fine woolly undercoat in winter. Sheds naturally with increasing day length and temperature. Should be free of permanent wool fibers in purebreds. Any color/pattern. |
| **Growth Rate** | ADG (purebred): ~0.30 kg/d (~0.66 lb/d). ADG (Suffolk-sired crosses): ~0.38 kg/d (~0.84 lb/d) (Maierle, 2018). 60-day weight (twins): 40--55 lbs. 90-day weight: 45--75 lbs (varies with nutrition/season). Market weight 95--115 lbs at 6--8 months. Birth weight (twins): ~8 lbs. |
| **Breed-All-Year** | Yes, extended breeding season. Pregnancy rates >80% in all seasons (Nov, July, March) at VSU (37 deg N). Peak fertility in fall. Rams generally fertile year-round. Suited to accelerated lambing (3 crops/2 years). |
| **Carcass Quality** | Lean, well-muscled. Dressing % comparable to medium-sized maternal breeds. Carcass weight and loin muscle area intermediate -- greater than St. Croix/Barbados Blackbelly, less than Dorper and Suffolk. Mild flavor, tender. |
| **Foot Rot Resistance** | Increased resistance reported compared to wool breeds. Hair sheep breeds including Katahdin showed fewer affected foot locations in Virginia Tech virulent footrot trials (Burke et al., 2003). |
| **Unique Traits** | NSIP genetic evaluation widely available with FEC EBVs for parasite resistance selection. USA HAIR Index combines growth, milk, prolificacy, survival. Largest registered hair sheep breed in US. Genome-wide association identified loci on chromosomes 2, 3, 16, 23, 24 for GIN resistance (Benavides et al., 2020). |

## 2. BARBADOS BLACKBELLY

| Trait | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mature Weight** | Rams: 90--150 lbs (avg 100--130); Ewes: 70--120 lbs (avg 85--100). Slow maturing -- ideal weight not reached until ~2 years. US animals tend larger than Caribbean stock due to better nutrition. (BBSA; Oklahoma State) |
| **Parasite Resistance** | High. Yazwinski et al. (1979): Barbados Blackbelly and BB crosses had significantly lower total FEC than Dorset, D x Landrace, and R x Landrace groups. Higher hemoglobin and MCHC levels. Virginia Tech: Caribbean hair sheep (St. Croix x BB) had lower FEC than Katahdins in some years. Coat lanolin helps repel ectoparasites. |
| **Prolificacy** | High. Lambing rate 150--230% (varies by study). Mature ewes commonly produce twins or triplets. Can lamb twice/year under good management. In Tobago study: flock avg litter size 1.50--2.30. |
| **Mothering Ability** | Excellent. Strong maternal bonds. Ewes actively defend lambs -- will charge dogs/humans. Good milk production. Pre-weaning mortality ~18.3% in Tobago study (Rastogi et al., 2002). Vigorous lambs. |
| **Temperament** | Flighty and alert. Retains wild ancestor wariness. Does not enjoy handling. More flighty than Dorper, Katahdin, and St. Croix. Reactive to dogs/cats. Challenging for routine handling. |
| **Flocking Instinct** | Strong. Exceptionally strong flocking instinct -- individuals show stress when separated. Form tight-knit groups. Can use flocking behavior to guide into working areas rather than catching individuals. |
| **Heat Tolerance** | Excellent. Evolved in tropical Caribbean. Sleek hair coat prevents overheating. Thrives in hot, humid conditions challenging for most sheep. Can survive on poor-quality tropical grasses. |
| **Cold Tolerance** | Moderate. Adaptable to colder climates. Grows short winter wool undercoat in cold climates that sheds in spring. Not ideal for extreme cold but manages well with shelter. |
| **Coat Type** | 100% hair (no wool) in tropical origin. Coarse hair lies flat against skin. Contains lanolin to shed water. In colder US climates, develops protective wool undercoat that sheds in spring. Never sheared. |
| **Growth Rate** | Slow. ADG: 0.27 lb/d in pen feeding (Burke et al., 2003); 0.38--0.45 lb/d weight-per-day-of-age at 5--7 months on alfalfa/wheat rations (BBSA). ADG on alfalfa: 0.38 lb/d; on fescue: 0.32 lb/d. 56-day weight: ~10.8 kg (~24 lbs). 6-month weight: ~19.2 kg (~42 lbs). Birth weight: 2.75--2.99 kg (~6--6.6 lbs). 60--70% of expected gain for wool sheep on similar rations. |
| **Breed-All-Year** | Yes. Non-seasonal breeders. Can breed and lamb year-round. Highest annual lambing rate (93%) among three hair breeds tested at VSU (vs. Katahdin 85%, St. Croix 77%). Capable of 3 litters per 2 years. |
| **Carcass Quality** | Lean. Carcass weight commonly 30--40 lbs. Dressing % highest (P<0.05) among hair breeds in one study (tied with Katahdin). Fat over ribeye: 1.5--2 mm (vs. 5--6 mm Suffolk/Dorset crosses). KPH fat: 0.75--1% (vs. 2.5--3% Suffolk/Dorset). Ribeye area: 2.0--2.4 sq in per 50 lbs carcass. Very lean, mild-flavored meat. |
| **Foot Rot Resistance** | Increased resistance reported. Hair sheep breeds including Blackbelly have reported increased footrot resistance (Frontiers in Genetics GWAS, 2023). |
| **Unique Traits** | Among the best out-of-season breeders. Ewes with young lambs show protective behavior (raising front feet to strike). Small US population, especially polled stock. Breed conservation concern. Contributed to development of Katahdin and Virginia Tech hair sheep composites. |

## 3. AMERICAN BLACKBELLY

| Trait | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mature Weight** | Rams: 100--160 lbs; Ewes: 70--120 lbs. Ram height 30--32 in at withers; Ewe height 24--28 in. Slow growing; ideal weight not reached until ~2 years. (BBSA; Oklahoma State) |
| **Parasite Resistance** | High (comparable to Barbados Blackbelly parentage). Often parasite resistant as is the case with several hair sheep breeds. Inherits tropical hair sheep parasite tolerance from Barbados Blackbelly foundation. |
| **Prolificacy** | ~200%. Doubles/triplets common; quadruplets not rare; occasional quintuplets. Mature ewes generally 2--3+ lambs/lambing. Capable of 3 litters per 1.5 years. |
| **Mothering Ability** | Very good. Produce good quantities of milk. Bond well with lambs. Lamb with minimal problems and intervention. |
| **Temperament** | Similar to Barbados Blackbelly -- alert and flighty. More wary of humans than Katahdin or Dorper. Strong survival instincts from Mouflon ancestry. Exotic appearance with impressive horns in rams. |
| **Flocking Instinct** | Strong. Described as having strong flocking instinct (BBSA breed standard). |
| **Heat Tolerance** | Excellent. Inherits Caribbean hair sheep heat adaptation. Thrives in hot, humid climates. |
| **Cold Tolerance** | Moderate-good. Grows more or less winter wool in response to local conditions. Entirely cast in spring/summer to reveal coarse, flat hair coat. |
| **Coat Type** | Hair coat. Grows variable winter wool depending on climate, entirely shed in spring/summer. Antelope-like markings. Never docked or sheared. Brown coat (light fawn to deep mahogany) with black underbelly and markings. |
| **Growth Rate** | Slow growing (similar to Barbados Blackbelly). Specific ADG data not separately published from BB. Produces small-to-medium lean carcasses. |
| **Breed-All-Year** | Yes. Non-seasonal. Out-of-season breeding (all year) per UF/IFAS VM264. Suited to accelerated lambing programs. |
| **Carcass Quality** | Small, lean carcasses. Produces fine-grained, mild meat. Attractive to grass-fed/specialty ethnic markets. Slower growth and lighter carcass than improved meat breeds. |
| **Foot Rot Resistance** | Likely similar to Barbados Blackbelly -- increased resistance compared to wool breeds. No breed-specific studies found. |
| **Unique Traits** | Developed in Texas by crossing Barbados Blackbelly x Rambouillet x Mouflon. Rams are horned (often spectacular 30+ inch curls). Exotic appearance used for both meat production and biological weed management. Distinct breed since 2004 (name formally adopted). |

## 4. ST. AUGUSTINE (FLORIDA NATIVE COMPOSITE)

| Trait | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mature Weight** | Rams: 180--200 lbs; Ewes: 130--150 lbs. Medium-sized frame. (St. Augustine Hair Sheep Association Breed Standard) |
| **Parasite Resistance** | Good. Inherits resistance from St. Croix (5/8) parentage. Well-adapted to hot, humid Florida conditions. Specific FEC data not published for this composite breed separately. |
| **Prolificacy** | High. Annual lambing: 190% lamb crop. Accelerated program (3x/2 yrs): 175% per lambing. Yearling ewes at first lambing: 150%. Multiple births common. Birth weight 7--8 lbs (mature ewes), 5--6 lbs (yearlings). |
| **Mothering Ability** | Excellent maternal ability. Deep-bodied ewes produce multiple births with ease. Lambs are vigorous with good nursing instincts. Early maturing ewes. |
| **Temperament** | Easy to handle. Developed specifically for ease of management in Florida. |
| **Flocking Instinct** | Good flocking instinct (St. Augustine Hair Sheep Association). |
| **Heat Tolerance** | Excellent. Developed in Florida specifically for hot, humid conditions. Rams fertile during hot summer months when other breeds are sterile. Ewes/rams do well on coastal/bahia grass pasture. Ears medium to large to promote cooling. |
| **Cold Tolerance** | Moderate. Not specifically developed for cold climates. Florida-adapted breed. |
| **Coat Type** | Variable. Three coat types: (1) 100% hair with no permanent wool; (2) short wool/hair combination that sheds; (3) wool/hair combination that does NOT shed. Selection should favor shedding types. Any color/pattern. |
| **Growth Rate** | Improved over St. Croix parent due to 3/8 Dorper genetics. Lambs grow well. Specific ADG numbers not published. Market lambs targeted for ethnic/specialty markets. |
| **Breed-All-Year** | Yes. Rams fertile year-round including summer. Non-seasonal breeding. Suited to accelerated lambing (3 crops/2 years). Ewe lambs can be bred at 7 months. |
| **Carcass Quality** | Dressing percentage: 50%. Mild-flavored meat. Medium frame provides improved carcass over pure St. Croix. |
| **Foot Rot Resistance** | Not specifically documented. Likely inherits some resistance from St. Croix parentage. |
| **Unique Traits** | Developed by Ron Taber in Florida starting 1991, named 2000. 5/8 St. Croix x 3/8 Dorper. Combines St. Croix hardiness/parasite resistance with Dorper frame/muscling. Roman nose common. Relatively new breed -- small population. Ron Taber died 2004; work continued by Ruth Taber and others. Calovine Farm (Williston, FL) is a key breeding operation. |

## 5. DORPER (BLACK HEADED DORPER)

| Trait | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mature Weight** | Rams: 230--270 lbs; Ewes: 180--210 lbs. Lambs reach ~80 lbs (36 kg) at 3--4 months. (ADSBS; Oklahoma State; ILRI) |
| **Parasite Resistance** | Low. Developed in arid South Africa -- exhibited little parasite resistance. FEC 62% higher than Katahdin in Virginia Tech trials. Highest FEC among hair breeds tested. However, shows RESILIENCE (maintains PCV and body weight despite high worm burden). Requires more intensive monitoring in humid climates. FAMACHA heritability in Dorper: 0.19. (Burke et al., 2003; Virginia Tech) |
| **Prolificacy** | Moderate-high. Lambing rate 150--180%. Avg litter size 1.19--1.5. Twinning rate ~10--24% depending on study. Triplets occasional. Lambing interval ~8 months. Can produce 2.25 lambs annually under accelerated management. (Cloete et al., 2000; ADSBS) |
| **Mothering Ability** | Good. Easy lambing. Good mothering and fertility. Ewes produce for 7+ years. (Cornell Small Farms; ADSBS) |
| **Temperament** | Calm, even temperament. "Vigorous appearance" is ideal (SA breed standard). Mature rams can be aggressive. Occasional "jumping" behavior in tight handling situations. Generally docile. (Cloete et al., 2000; ADSBS) |
| **Flocking Instinct** | Moderate-good. Gregarious. Manageable in groups. |
| **Heat Tolerance** | Good. Developed for arid South Africa. Adapts well globally. Thick skin (marketed as "Cape Glovers") provides protection. However, originated in DRY heat -- may be less adapted to HUMID heat than Caribbean-origin breeds. UF/IFAS notes may require more intensive monitoring in Florida. |
| **Cold Tolerance** | Good. Grows thicker coat in winter. Thick skin provides insulation. Successfully raised in sub-zero temperatures (New York). Needs windbreak/shelter for extreme conditions. |
| **Coat Type** | Mixed hair/wool. Short, loose, light mixture of hair and wool. Should shed covering each summer. Shedding ability is variable and must be selected for. Some individuals require shearing on back/hindquarters. "Shedding happens from the bottom up." Breed standard: all-hair or all-wool is a CULL. (ADSBS) |
| **Growth Rate** | Fast. ADG: 243 g/d (0.54 lb/d) average. 36 kg (~80 lbs) at 3--4 months. Post-weaning ADG: 239 g/d for F1 Dorper (Snowder & Duckett, 2003). Under extensive conditions: 81--91 g/d (Campbell). Faster than Katahdin and St. Croix at all stages. (Cloete et al., 2000; USDA) |
| **Breed-All-Year** | Yes. Long breeding season, not seasonally limited. Can lamb 3x in 2 years. Dorper ewes achieved 87--94% conception rates across different seasons. (ADSBS; TAMUK study) |
| **Carcass Quality** | Excellent. Dressing %: ~50%, up to 54%. Carcass ~16 kg at 3--4 months. Most similar to wool breed carcasses in weight and muscling. Warner-Bratzler shear force values lower (more tender) than many breeds. Tendency to deposit more external fat at lighter weights (early maturing). Thick skin is world's most sought-after sheepskin. (Cloete et al., 2000; Snowder & Duckett, 2003) |
| **Foot Rot Resistance** | Minimal advantage. In Virginia Tech footrot trial: Dorper crosses showed similar or slightly more affected foot locations than Katahdin and St. Croix. (Burke et al.) |
| **Unique Traits** | Dorset Horn x Blackhead Persian cross (South Africa, 1940--50). World's most valuable sheepskin ("Cape Glovers"). Early maturing terminal sire breed. Fat deposition greater than other hair breeds -- manage slaughter timing carefully. Not a true hair sheep (UF/IFAS) but commonly used in hair sheep crossbreeding. |

## 6. BLACK HEADED DORPER vs. WHITE DORPER

The American Dorper Sheep Breeders' Society and the South African Dorper Breeders' Society state that **the color pattern is the ONLY characteristic where there are different standards for Dorpers and White Dorpers**. All production data (weight, growth, carcass, reproduction) applies equally to both types.

| Trait | Black Headed Dorper | White Dorper |
|---|---|---|
| **Appearance** | White body with black head and neck. Limited spots acceptable on body/legs. | All white. Full pigmentation required on eyelids, under tail, reproductive organs. |
| **Origin** | Dorset Horn x Blackhead Persian (1942) | Dorset Horn x Blackhead Persian + Dorset Horn x Van Rooy. Merged with Dorper society in 1964. |
| **All other traits** | Identical to Dorper data above | Identical to Dorper data above |
| **Pigmentation note** | Complete pigmentation on anus, reproductive organs, hooves required. | Eyelid and reproductive organ pigmentation critical for sun protection. |

One breeder noted White Dorper lambing percentage averages about 180%, which is at the higher end of the Dorper range but not a confirmed breed-type difference.

## 7. GULF COAST NATIVE

| Trait | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mature Weight** | Rams: 125--200 lbs; Ewes: 90--160 lbs. Small, fine-boned. Often much smaller on poor grazing land. Height: 30--48 in. (Livestock Conservancy; GCSBA) |
| **Parasite Resistance** | Very high -- among the highest of all breeds. Had 1/8th the FEC of Suffolk sheep under similar conditions (Haemonchus contortus, Ostertagia, Trichostrongylus spp.). UF flock maintained 30+ years without dewormers. Some flocks maintained for many years without any anthelmintic treatment. (Livestock Conservancy; USDA ARS) |
| **Prolificacy** | Moderate. 70% singles, 30% twins, rarely triplets. 150% annual lamb crop. Can produce 3 lamb crops in 2 years. Incidence of multiple births increases with good forage. (Livestock Conservancy; GCSBA) |
| **Mothering Ability** | Excellent. Ewes pasture-lamb without assistance. Vigorous lambs that grow rapidly. Good natural mothering instinct. |
| **Temperament** | Alert and docile. Suitable for novice to intermediate shepherds. Described as "smart, intuitive, gentle, personable." Can be somewhat independent from semi-feral heritage. (GCSBA; Livestock Conservancy) |
| **Flocking Instinct** | Good. Strong natural flocking behavior. Good flocking noted as a breed characteristic. Semi-feral heritage contributed to natural group cohesion for predator defense. (Livestock Conservancy; Florida Cracker Sheep Association) |
| **Heat Tolerance** | Excellent. Evolved in Gulf Coast heat/humidity over centuries. Temperatures >100 deg F do not interfere with breeding. Bare faces, legs, bellies adapted for heat dissipation. (GCSBA; Livestock Conservancy) |
| **Cold Tolerance** | Poor-moderate. Not considered cold-hardy. Best suited to southeastern US warm/humid environments. May adapt with thicker fleece growth in colder areas but not ideal. |
| **Coat Type** | Light WOOL (not hair). Soft wool, staple 2.5--4 in. Usually white; occasional brown, black, or spotted. No wool on face, belly, or legs (heat adaptation). Raw fleece weight small. Some hair may be intermixed. Not a hair sheep -- this is a wooled breed. (Livestock Conservancy; GCSBA) |
| **Growth Rate** | Slow-moderate. Formal published ADG and growth data is very limited for this breed. Lambs described as "vigorous and grow rapidly" but no specific numbers widely published. Slower growth and smaller carcasses than improved meat breeds. |
| **Breed-All-Year** | Yes. Breed nearly year-round. Temperatures >100 deg F do not interfere with breeding. Capable of 3 lamb crops in 2 years. |
| **Carcass Quality** | Limited formal data. Small to medium carcasses. Lean. Listed on Slow Food USA's Ark of Taste (recognized for flavor). No formal USDA carcass grade data widely published. |
| **Foot Rot Resistance** | Excellent -- well-documented resistance to foot rot. This is a distinctive breed characteristic. Many breeders and research universities confirm resistance. Merino-derived breeds most susceptible; GCN among most resistant. (Livestock Conservancy; GCSBA) |
| **Unique Traits** | One of oldest sheep breeds in North America (Spanish origin, 1500s). CRITICALLY ENDANGERED -- conservation priority. Slow Food Ark of Taste. DNA analysis confirms distinct from Florida Cracker. UF research flock maintained 30+ years without dewormers. Crossbreeding trials (LSU) showed F1 offspring hardy/parasite resistant, but resistance diluted in subsequent generations. May be polled or horned in both sexes. |

## 8. FLORIDA CRACKER (FLORIDA CRACKER SHEEP)

| Trait | Data |
|---|---|
| **Mature Weight** | Rams: 135--200 lbs; Ewes: 85--135 lbs. Medium size, light-boned, long-legged. Natural lean appearance -- should NOT appear bulky/obese. (Florida Cracker Sheep Association; Livestock Conservancy) |
| **Parasite Resistance** | Very high. Mean FEC of 1,427 eggs/g (vs. Merino mean of 3,281 under selection for resistance). Heritability of FEC: h2 = 0.33; PCV: h2 = 0.22; FAMACHA: h2 = 0.31 (higher than Merino 0.24 and Dorper 0.19). Genetic correlation FEC-to-FAMACHA: 0.51. 15 SNPs across 10 chromosomes identified for resistance markers. Dramatic phenotype differences: resistant animals 150 FEC/FAMACHA 2 vs. susceptible 20,000 FEC/FAMACHA 5. (Estrada-Reyes et al., 2019, 2021, 2022; UF; Frontiers in Animal Science 2023) |
| **Prolificacy** | Moderate-high. Twins common; triplets and quadruplets occasional. Can breed back 1 month post-lambing. Good ewes can produce 2 lamb crops/year. Lambing rates respond well to selection. (Livestock Conservancy; FCSA) |
| **Mothering Ability** | Excellent. Good milk production. High weaning rates. Excellent mothering instincts noted as key breed characteristic. High lamb survivability. |
| **Temperament** | Alert and docile. Active and vigorous without tendency to be wild. Gentle nature. Head held moderately high, ears alert, legs squarely placed. Suitable for novice to intermediate shepherds. (FCSA breed standard) |
| **Flocking Instinct** | Good. Good flocking ability cited as breed characteristic. (Livestock Conservancy; FCSA) |
| **Heat Tolerance** | Excellent. Adapted for ~500 years to humid subtropical Florida. Can survive heat, humidity, high winds, poor forage, drought. This is the breed's main strength. |
| **Cold Tolerance** | Poor-moderate. Florida-adapted breed. Not specifically cold tolerant. Wool provides some insulation but breed selected for warm/humid conditions. |
| **Coat Type** | WOOL (not hair). Low-density fleece, white to black in color. Wool on body; short hair on legs, belly, behind ears, and head. Raw fleece weight 3--3.5 lbs with 47--57% yield. Varied colors and patterns (more color diversity than Gulf Coast Native). Landrace breed with greater diversity of appearance. (Livestock Conservancy; FCSA) |
| **Growth Rate** | Slow. "Lambs often are slow growing." Carcass traits historically inferior to other meat breeds (small and lean). Specific ADG numbers studied in UF research (phenotyped as part of parasite resistance studies) but breed-specific ADG benchmarks not widely published. |
| **Breed-All-Year** | Yes. Can breed back 1 month after lambing. Good ewes produce 2 lamb crops/year. Year-round breeding capability. |
| **Carcass Quality** | Small and lean. Historically inferior carcass traits compared to improved meat breeds. Mild meat flavor. No formal dressing percentage data published. Lean carcass. |
| **Foot Rot Resistance** | Good. Strong parasite resistance extends to foot rot based on breed characteristics and adaptation to wet Florida conditions. Not specifically quantified in comparative trials. |
| **Unique Traits** | One of oldest breeds in North America (~500 years from Spanish Churra). CRITICALLY ENDANGERED (fewer than 1,000 animals). DNA analysis confirms DISTINCT from Gulf Coast Native. Formerly called "Florida Native" and "Florida Scrub Sheep." UF/Fort Valley State genomic research identified immune genes (CCL1, CCL2, CCL8, CCL11, NOS2, TNF, CSF3, STAT3, WC1.1 Antigen/gamma-delta T cells) for H. contortus resistance. Largest commercial flock at Fairmeadow Sheep Farm, Ocala, FL. Landrace breed -- greater phenotypic diversity than standardized breeds. |

## 9. IMPORTANT NOTE: FLORIDA CRACKER vs. GULF COAST NATIVE

These two breeds share Spanish colonial ancestry but are genetically DISTINCT populations confirmed by DNA analysis. Key distinctions:

| Trait | Florida Cracker | Gulf Coast Native |
|---|---|---|
| **Coat** | Wool, varied colors common | Wool, usually white |
| **DNA** | Distinct population (UF analysis) | Distinct population |
| **Foot/hoof/belly wool** | Short hair on legs/belly/head | No wool on face/belly/legs |
| **Conservation** | Critical (<1,000 animals) | Critical |
| **Home range** | Florida specifically | Gulf Coast states broadly |
| **Research base** | UF genomic parasite research | LSU, USDA ARS research |
