# COMPREHENSIVE SHEEP BREED DATA REPORT FOR A FLORIDA FLOCK

## Important Florida Context

Per UF/IFAS Extension publication VM264 ("Selection of Sheep Meat Breeds in Florida"), Florida's wet, humid subtropical climate presents severe challenges for sheep, particularly from *Haemonchus contortus* (barber pole worm), which thrives in warm, moist conditions. UF/IFAS generally recommends **hair sheep breeds** (Katahdin, Florida Cracker/Native, St. Croix) for Florida due to superior heat tolerance and parasite resistance. The breeds below are wool and dual-purpose breeds, which will require more intensive management in Florida.

## KEY RECOMMENDATIONS FOR YOUR FLORIDA FLOCK

Based on the research, among these 10 wool/dual-purpose breeds:

1. **Best suited for Florida:** **Wiltshire Horn** (self-shedding, good parasite resistance, good heat tolerance, good prolificacy) and **Tunis** (heat-tolerant, year-round breeding, parasite resistance, docile, feed-efficient).

2. **Promising with caveats:** **Karakul** and **Awassi** have excellent heat tolerance but are adapted to DRY heat, not Florida's humidity. Both have good parasite resistance and out-of-season breeding. The Awassi's milk production is a major asset. Monitor closely for humidity-related issues.

3. **Workable with management:** **Jacob** (hardy, parasite-resistant, light fleece, small) and **Babydoll** (docile, foot-rot resistant, but needs shade and careful heat management).

4. **Challenging for Florida:** **Hampshire** and **Suffolk** (excellent for meat/growth but poor heat tolerance, parasite-susceptible, seasonal breeders). Best used only as terminal sires in strategic crosses.

5. **Not recommended for Florida:** **Cotswold** (massive fleece, slow growth, no heat adaptation) and **East Friesian** (very susceptible to parasites and heat stress, pneumonia-prone). East Friesians should only be used through crossbreeding with adapted breeds.

Per UF/IFAS, regardless of breed choice, Florida sheep producers should implement FAMACHA scoring, fecal egg count monitoring, rotational grazing, and strategic targeted selective treatment (TST) for parasites.
