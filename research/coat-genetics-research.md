# Hair vs. Wool Coat Genetics in Sheep: Comprehensive Research Summary

## 1. GENETICS OF HAIR vs. WOOL COAT

### 1a. Genetic Architecture: Major Gene + Polygenic

The evidence strongly supports a **mixed inheritance model**: one (or a few) major genes plus polygenic modifiers.

**Pollott (2011)** tested four modes of inheritance (autosomal recessive, sex-linked recessive, autosomal dominant, sex-linked dominant) in Easycare/Wiltshire Horn/Katahdin/Dorper composites. The result: **autosomal dominant** inheritance was the most likely mode (P < 0.05), with a low level of incomplete penetrance. In first backcross animals, dominance was confirmed with complete penetrance.

Pollott proposed a **two-part genetic model**:
1. **The ability to shed** -- controlled by a major autosomal dominant gene (you either have it or you don't)
2. **The extent/completeness of shedding** -- a polygenic quantitative trait (how well and how fast they shed)

**Matika et al. (2013)** confirmed this in Easycare sheep (565 animals, 2002-2010). Complex segregation analysis found heritability of 0.80 +/- 0.06 with evidence for a **single putative dominant gene**. They identified 4 significant SNPs, one of which showed dominance effects consistent with the segregation analysis.

**Practical implication**: Hair shedding is NOT purely additive based on breed percentage. There is a major dominant gene -- meaning a 50% hair sheep cross may shed much better than a simple 50% average would predict, IF the dominant shedding allele is inherited. But extent of shedding is then polygenic/quantitative on top of that.

### 1b. Candidate Genes

**Structural/Keratin genes:**
- KRT14, KRT17, KRT25, KRT27, KRT71, KRT74 (keratin family)
- KAP genes (keratin-associated proteins)

**Hair follicle development/cycling:**
- TMEM79 (transmembrane protein -- hair follicle morphogenesis)
- HOXC13, MSX2, DBI, FZD3, ZDHHC21, DSG4 (Dorper shedding study, 2024)
- WNT10A, SOX18, SOX10, FZD1, BMP7 (Wnt/BMP signaling pathway)
- PRX, TGM3, TCF3 (selective signature analysis, 27 breeds)

**Follicle biology basis:** The key physical difference between hair and wool sheep is the **secondary-to-primary follicle ratio (S/P ratio)**:
- Hair sheep and primitive breeds: S/P ratio of ~1-3
- Wool breeds: S/P ratio of 5-20+
- Merino (extreme wool): S/P ratio up to 20

### 1c. Scoring Systems

| System | Scale | Direction | Used By |
|--------|-------|-----------|---------|
| Pollott (2011) | 0-5 | 0 = no shedding, 5 = complete shed | UK research |
| Dolling (2003) | 1-7 | 1 = complete shed, 7 = no shedding | Australian research |
| Signet (UK) | 1-9 | 1 = not shedding, 9 = fully shed | UK industry (AHDB) |
| NZ Breeders | 0-10 | 0 = no shedding, 10 = fully shed | New Zealand industry |
| KHSI Registry | AA/A/B/C | AA = best shed, C = fail | US Katahdin registration |
| CKSA (Canada) | AA/A/B/C | Same as KHSI | Canadian Katahdin registration |
| Matika (2013) | 0-9 | 0 = full fleece retained, 9 = completely shed | UK Easycare research |

**KHSI Coat Grades (for registration):**
- **AA**: Naturally free of visible woolly fibers at all times; sheds completely
- **A**: Some evidence of woolly fibers, but seasonally sheds completely
- **B**: Some patches do not shed, total area not exceeding 1/4 of upper body
- **C**: Fails coat inspection -- can be "recorded" but not registered

## 2. PREDICTING COAT TYPE IN CROSSES

### 2a. Hair x Wool Crosses (e.g., Katahdin x Hampshire)

**F1 generation (50% hair / 50% wool):**
- Will have wool fleeces with interspersed hair
- Will require shearing
- Fleeces should be discarded (unmarketable)
- The dominant shedding gene may be present in ~50% of F1s, but wool density is too high for clean shedding

**Backcross to hair (75% hair / 25% wool):**
- May or may not require shearing depending on breed
- Significant variation expected

**Second backcross (87.5% hair / 12.5% wool):**
- Most animals should not require shearing
- F3s at 87.5% Katahdin can be registered as Katahdins after passing coat inspection

**Rule of thumb from KHSI**: It takes **3 generations of backcrossing to purebred Katahdin rams** to eliminate the need for shearing.

### 2b. Hair x Hair Crosses (e.g., Katahdin x Dorper)

This is NOT a guaranteed clean outcome. The Dorper is technically a "shedding breed" rather than a "true hair breed."

**Katahdin x Dorper F1:**
- Most will shed, but many will retain woolly patches
- First-year lambs are often "fluffballs" that don't shed cleanly
- White Dorper crosses tend to shed better than standard (blackhead) Dorper crosses

**Katahdin x St. Croix or Barbados Blackbelly:**
- Both parents are true hair sheep with the dominant shedding gene
- Offspring will have clean hair coats
- Minimal wool retention expected

### 2c. Predictive Framework

**Step 1: Shedding Potential Score (SPS) by Breed (0-5 scale):**

| Breed | SPS (0-5) | Classification |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| Barbados Blackbelly | 5.0 | Pure hair sheep |
| St. Croix | 5.0 | Pure hair sheep |
| American Blackbelly | 5.0 | Pure hair sheep |
| Katahdin | 4.5 | Improved hair sheep |
| Wiltshire Horn | 4.0 | Shedding breed (wool-type fibers that shed) |
| St Augustine | 4.0 | 5/8 St. Croix, 3/8 Dorper composite |
| Dorper (White) | 3.0 | Shedding breed with significant wool |
| Dorper (Standard) | 2.5 | More wool retention than White Dorper |
| Gulf Coast Native | 1.0 | Wool breed (light, some hair) |
| Florida Cracker | 1.0 | Wool breed (light, some hair) |
| Tunis | 0.5 | Medium wool, down-type |
| Hampshire | 0.5 | Medium wool, down-type |
| Suffolk | 0.5 | Medium wool, down-type |
| Babydoll Southdown | 0.5 | Medium-fine wool, down-type |
| Jacob | 0.5 | Medium wool |
| East Friesian | 0.5 | Medium wool, dairy breed |
| Cotswold | 0.0 | Long wool, heavy fleece |
| Karakul | 0.5 | Double-coated (guard hair + undercoat), carpet wool |
| Awassi | 0.5 | Carpet wool, high kemp/hair content |

**Step 2: Two-component model:**

**Component A -- Shedding ability (binary/dominant):**
- Probability of inheriting the dominant shedding allele from a parent breed
- Pure hair breeds (SPS >= 4.5): assume homozygous (SS), probability of passing = 1.0
- Shedding breeds (SPS 3.0-4.0): assume heterozygous mix (Ss/SS), probability ~0.75-0.90
- Wool breeds (SPS <= 1.0): assume homozygous non-shedding (ss), probability = 0.0

**Component B -- Extent of shedding (polygenic/quantitative):**
- Use a weighted average of parent SPS values
- Apply heritability correction: offspring_SPS = population_mean + h^2 * (midparent_SPS - population_mean)
- Use h^2 = 0.50 (mid-range of published estimates)

**Combined prediction:**
```
P(sheds) = probability from Component A
Expected_shedding_extent = Component B value
Predicted_score = P(sheds) * Expected_shedding_extent
```

**Step 3: Generational backcrossing formula:**

```
Hair_fraction(n) = 1 - (1 - Hair_fraction_initial) * 0.5^n
```

Expected need for shearing:
- Hair fraction < 0.75: likely needs shearing
- Hair fraction 0.75-0.87: may need shearing (breed-dependent)
- Hair fraction > 0.87: should not need shearing
- Hair fraction > 0.94: hair coat effectively fixed

## 3. HAIR SHEDDING SCORES / COAT TYPE BY BREED

### True Hair Sheep (Score 5/5 -- complete shedding)

**Barbados Blackbelly:** Pure hair sheep, no wool. Coarse hair lies flat. Rams have chest/neck mane. In cold climates develops woolly undercoat that sheds completely in spring.

**St. Croix:** Pure hair sheep, white. Coarse outer coat + finer undercoat. Sheds completely. Rams develop lion-like mane reaching to knees.

**American Blackbelly:** Hair sheep (Barbados Blackbelly x Mouflon x Rambouillet cross). Coarse hair lies flat, rams have black mane. Horned rams (unlike polled Barbados Blackbelly).

### Improved Hair Sheep (Score 4-4.5/5)

**Katahdin:** Coat is mixture of hair and wool fibers that sheds completely. Winter coat thickness varies by individual. Must pass KHSI coat inspection (A or B grade) for registration.

**St. Augustine:** 5/8 St. Croix, 3/8 Dorper composite developed in Florida. Most have hair that starts shedding in February. Some retain strip of wool on back from Dorper genetics.

### Shedding Breeds (Score 2.5-4/5)

**Wiltshire Horn:** British breed. Short "kemp wool" that sheds annually in spring. NOT a true hair coat. No wool on belly, chest underside, head, front legs, lower hind legs. Autosomal dominant shedding gene.

**Dorper (White):** Not a true hair sheep. ~10% may need shearing even within the breed. White Dorpers tend to shed better than standard Dorpers.

**Dorper (Standard/Blackhead):** More wool retention than White Dorper.

### Wool Breeds with Some Hair (Score 0.5-1.0)

**Gulf Coast Native:** WOOL breed, not hair. Light wool, 2.5-4 inch staple, may have some hair mixed in.

**Florida Cracker:** WOOL breed. Wool on body, shorter hair on legs/belly/behind ears. 24-34 micron, 2.5-3 inch staple.

**Karakul:** Double-coated: coarse guard hair outer coat + fine down undercoat. Carpet wool. Does NOT shed.

**Awassi:** Fat-tail breed, carpet wool with high kemp/hair content. 24% hair content in fleece. Does NOT shed.

### Pure Wool Breeds (Score 0-0.5/5)

**Hampshire:** Down-type, 25-33 micron, 6-10 lb fleece. NO shedding.

**Suffolk:** Down-type, 25.5-33 micron, 5-8 lb fleece. NO shedding.

**Cotswold:** Luster longwool. 33-40 micron, 13-15 lb fleece. NO shedding.

**Tunis:** Down-type, 24-30 micron, 8-10 lb fleece. NO shedding.

**Jacob:** Medium grade, 25-35 micron, 3-6 lb fleece. Piebald. NO shedding.

**Babydoll Southdown:** Fine wool, 19-22 microns. Dense, tight fleece. NO shedding.

**East Friesian:** Medium wool, 26-37 micron, 8.8-11 lb fleece. Distinctive wool-free "rat tail." NO shedding.

## 4. HERITABILITY OF HAIR SHEDDING

### Published h-squared Estimates

| Study | Population | h-squared | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Matika et al. 2013 | Easycare sheep | **0.80 +/- 0.06** | Continuous trait; also 0.65-0.75 as binary |
| Pollott 2011 | Easycare/Wiltshire/Katahdin/Dorper | **0.54 +/- 0.07** (lambs), **0.26 +/- 0.06** (all ages) | Lower in adults due to environmental effects |
| Vargas Jurado et al. 2020 | Romanov/WD/Katahdin composite (USMARC) | **0.47 +/- 0.03** (lambs), **0.59 +/- 0.04** (1 yr) | 4,971 lamb + 3,335 ewe records |
| Vargas Jurado et al. 2020 | Same, repeatability model | **0.50 +/- 0.03** (adult ewes) | Repeatability: 0.60 +/- 0.02 |
| NZ Wiltshire 2024 | NZ Wiltshire composites | **0.54 +/- 0.02** (lambs) | 20,653 records, 12 flocks |
| Guy et al. 2021 | Australian sheep | **0.50 +/- 0.05** (lamb shedding) | |
| French Romane introgression | Romane x Martinik Hair | **0.69** (lambs), **0.42** (yearling), **0.38 +/- 0.04** (extent) | After introgression program |

**Consensus range: h-squared = 0.35 to 0.80**

**Best working estimate: h-squared = 0.50**

### Genetic Correlations
- Lamb shedding score genetically correlated with adult shedding: **r_g = 0.86 to 0.94**
- Phenotypic correlation (lamb to 2-year-old): only **0.39**
- Repeatability (year to year in adults): **0.60**

### How Many Generations to "Fix" a Hair Coat?

From the French introgression study (Martinik Hair into Romane):
- After **4 backcross generations** + **8 generations of selection**: mean shedding extent reached 89.3% (SD = 17.9%)
- Genetic gain of **3 standard deviations** was achieved over 8 generations of selection
- Shedding ability increased 75.3%, shedding extent increased 145.7%

From KHSI practical guidance:
- **3 generations** of backcrossing to purebred Katahdin rams typically eliminates shearing need
- F3 (87.5% Katahdin) can be registered after passing coat inspection

## 5. NSIP HAIR SHEDDING EBV

NSIP does not currently publish a standalone "hair shedding score" EBV for sheep in the United States. Coat quality is handled separately through the KHSI coat inspection system (AA/A/B/C grades).

**Standard NSIP EBVs for Katahdins:**
- BWT, WWT, PWWT, MWWT (weights)
- WFEC, PFEC (fecal egg count -- parasite resistance)
- NLB, NLW (number born/weaned)

**UK Signet system** DOES produce shedding EBVs on a 1-9 scale for Easycare, Wiltshire Horn, Exlana, etc.
