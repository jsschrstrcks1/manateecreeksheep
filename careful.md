# Careful, Not Clever

**Version**: 1.0.0
**Created**: 2026-02-15
**Purpose**: Guardrail to enforce careful, methodical work over clever shortcuts — because these sheep depend on accurate records
**Priority**: CRITICAL — This skill overrides the impulse to optimize, batch, or shortcut

---

## The Rule

> **Be careful, not clever.**
> Careful means: verified, documented, faithful, honest.
> Clever means: fast, creative, assumed, unverified.
> When in doubt, be careful.
> Everything we do is for the glory of God and with integrity.

---

## Before Modifying Any Record

1. **Read the source first.** Never edit a sheep record you haven't cross-referenced against the images or data files.
2. **Understand what's there.** Don't assume you know the pen assignments, pedigree, or health history. Check.
3. **Check for consistency.** If adding a sire/dam relationship, verify the claim against multiple sources. If adding a tag number, confirm it matches the spiral notebook (highest authority).
4. **State your assumptions.** Before a significant addition, list what you're assuming and verify each one.

## During Modifications

5. **One logical change at a time.** Don't combine unrelated record updates in a single pass.
6. **Verify every relationship.** If claiming Sheep A is the dam of Sheep B, confirm it in the source data. No guessing pedigrees.
7. **Verify every tag number.** Tags get reused, swapped, and lost. When sources conflict, the spiral notebook wins.
8. **Leave things alone when risk outweighs benefit.** If an addition could corrupt pedigree data, skip it. Say why you skipped it.
9. **Mark uncertainty.** Use `[UNCLEAR]` for any data point you can't verify. Set confidence to "low."

## After Modifications

10. **Verify, then report.** Don't say "done" until you've confirmed the records are consistent.
11. **Commit with honest messages.** Describe what was done AND what was intentionally left alone.
12. **Update CLAUDE.md when needed.** If a new pattern, alias, or flock management insight emerges, document it.

## What "Careful" Looks Like

- Cross-referencing a sheep's tag number across the spiral notebook, xlsx, and csv before recording it
- Verifying a dam-offspring relationship by checking lambing records, not assuming from pen proximity
- Saying "I couldn't verify this relationship, so I marked confidence as low" instead of guessing
- Committing after each logical unit of work, not batching everything at the end
- Preserving Mom's original notes and spellings in source_refs while normalizing names in the database

## What "Clever" Looks Like (Avoid)

- Guessing pedigree relationships based on breed composition alone
- Assuming two sheep with similar names are the same animal
- Inventing dates of birth when none are recorded
- Batching dozens of unrelated sheep record changes into one commit
- Making "improvements" to records without source verification
- Silently discarding data that doesn't fit your model

## The Integrity Test

Before every commit, ask yourself:

1. **Is every relationship verified?** Have I checked the source data, not just my assumptions?
2. **Is every tag number confirmed?** Could this be a different sheep with a similar tag?
3. **Does this addition serve the flock?** Or am I serving my own tidiness?
4. **Would this survive scrutiny from the shepherd?** If Ken or his mother looked at this, would it be right?
5. **Did I leave anything silently wrong?** If I'm not sure, mark it and move on.

## The Standard

These are living animals. A wrong pedigree leads to bad breeding decisions. A wrong health record leads to missed treatments. A wrong pen assignment leads to unwanted matings.

We don't guess. We verify.
We don't assume. We check.
We don't cut corners. We serve the flock.
